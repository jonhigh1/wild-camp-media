# Wild Camp Media — Competitor / Peer Analysis

Research date: 2026-08-25. Region treated as generic US regional. 12 sites evaluated across 4 niches. Mobile scores are inferred from platform and layout patterns (not rendered on-device) — flagged where uncertain.

---

## Individual Site Profiles

### 1. White Bison Media — whitebisonmedia.com (brewery/winery photo + video + virtual tours)
- **What they do:** Photography, video, drone, and Google virtual tours for wineries/breweries/hospitality. Ex-Google Trusted Photographer, FAA Part 107. Closest single analogue to Wild Camp Media.
- **Design:** Custom-feeling Squarespace-style, light, image-led. Sierra Nevada taproom hero.
- **Headline:** "Fill more seats. Sell more pours. Look like you mean it." — outcome-driven, blue-collar direct.
- **CTA:** Phone number + "Email for a Quote" in header; per-package "Get started" buttons; closes with call + email. Excellent.
- **Social proof:** Trust badges (Ex-Google, Part 107, nationwide, 5-day delivery) instead of logo wall; no testimonials on the brewery page — odd gap.
- **Pricing:** Fully public. $600 photo / $800 photo+tour / $2,500 video, plus a la carte add-on menu (twilight $100, FPV fly-through $250, aerial $200...). Also $200/mo website + SEO product. Best pricing page in the set.
- **Portfolio:** Inline galleries per service; no reel-first treatment.
- **Architecture:** ~6-8 pages (Industries, Hospitality, Services, Pricing, FAQ, Contact).
- **Notably good:** Problem-agitate copy ("Your taproom looks better in person than it does online. That is costing you money."), transparent pricing, delivery-speed guarantee.
- **Notably bad:** No client logos or named testimonials on this vertical page; photography-forward, not reel-first.

### 2. 21 Drones Media — 21dronesmedia.com (drone + websites, Rochester MN)

- **Notably bad:** Template look undercuts "high-end" claim; pricing anchors very low ($100/hr reads hobbyist).

### 3. Maken Media — maken.media (contractor video marketing, Scottsdale AZ)

- **Portfolio:** Case-study cards with one-line result ("driving consistent inbound leads"), not raw video grid.
- **Architecture:** Small nav (Services, Production, Blog, Contact) but deep blog — programmatic SEO play.
- **Notably good:** Outcome-led copy, stat counters, booking widget friction removal, pricing-adjacent blog content.
- **Notably bad:** No public pricing = friction for blue-collar buyers who hate calls; agency positioning drifts from "shooter for hire."

## 2. Patterns of the Top 10%

1. **Outcome headlines, not service descriptions.** Winners state what the client gets: "Fill more seats. Sell more pours." (White Bison), "We turn attention into leads. appointments. revenue." (Maken). Losers describe the service: "Drone Photography & Videography Services" (Pro Drones 360), "A Miami video production company committed to..." (ReyFilm).
2. **Numbers-as-proof stacks.** Maken: 400M+ views, 3,500+ videos, 5.0 stars. ReyFilm: 2,000+ projects, 15 years. Drone Brothers: 350+ pilots. The top sites convert credentials into specific, countable proof placed directly under the hero.
3. **Friction-removed CTAs.** Elite sites offer a scheduling widget (Calendly/GoHighLevel: Maken, t-lapse), a sticky click-to-call (Pro Drones 360), or purchasable packages (DroneVideos, 21 Drones). Average sites offer a bare contact form or — worst case — a mailto link (DGalMedia).
4. **Case cards with a result line, not bare video grids.** ReyFilm ("appointments up 46%"), Barnes ("leased 11K SF in under 90 days"), Maken ("consistent inbound leads"). The pattern is: client name, industry tag, one quantified outcome, then the work.
5. **Published pricing or pricing-adjacent content.** White Bison and 21 Drones publish full price menus; Maken publishes a "what does a videographer cost" article to capture the query while staying quote-based. Almost nobody else does either — the whole niche forces buyers into a call to learn price.

## 3. What Everyone Does Badly (Opportunities)

1. **Reel is not first.** Almost no competitor puts an autoplaying, sound-off showreel in the hero. Most bury video in grids or Vimeo links. A reel-first homepage instantly differentiates.
2. **Pricing opacity.** 9 of 12 sites show zero pricing. Blue-collar buyers (brewery owners, GCs) want a number or a range before calling. A visible "starting at" or 3-tier package page is a wide-open wedge — only White Bison and 21 Drones do it, and 21 Drones anchors too cheap.
3. **No multi-vertical proof organization.** Nobody segments proof by client type (construction vs brewery vs events) on the homepage, even though several serve mixed verticals. A "work by trade" filter is unclaimed.
4. **Testimonials without faces or outcomes.** Where testimonials exist (t-lapse, Drone Brothers) they are generic praise ("exceptional service"). Only Barnes and Maken attach business outcomes. Video testimonials from a GC and a brewery owner are effectively absent niche-wide.
5. **Dead or weak mobile CTAs.** Mailto links, phone numbers as plain text, and forms below 6 scroll-depth screens. Sticky call/text CTA is rare (only Pro Drones 360 does it well).
6. **Template smell + typos.** Wix/WordPress defaults with theme cruft footers (t-lapse), homepage typos (ReyFilm), and 2012-era design (AllProUSA). Craft execution alone beats half this market.
7. **Part 107 treated as a footnote.** Most mention certification in body copy. None make compliance + insurance a trust badge stack in the hero — despite it being a genuine purchase criterion for GCs and developers.
8. **No "process" page.** Only ReyFilm (3 steps) and Maken (6 steps) explain what happens after contact. For buyers new to hiring video, a 3-step "how it works" is cheap trust.

## 4. Homepage Headline Formulas Observed

**Formula 1 — Outcome promise (elite pattern).** *[Verb] + [business result].*
- "Fill more seats. Sell more pours. Look like you mean it." — White Bison Media
- "We turn attention into leads. appointments. revenue." — Maken Media
- Wild Camp adaptation: "Show the work. Win the job." / "Your build, on film. Your phone, ringing."

**Formula 2 — Credential/category declaration (average pattern).** *[Service] + [region] + [certifications].*
- "Drone Photography & Videography Services — FAA Part 107 Certified | Fully Insured | 100+ Projects Completed" — Pro Drones 360
- "PREMIUM CONTENT FOR PREMIUM CONTRACTORS" — DGalMedia (hybrid: positioning claim, no outcome)
- Works for search intent, converts poorly. Use as H2/supporting line, not H1.

**Formula 3 — Enterprise/status claim (premium pattern).** "[The] [niche]'s [superlative] [provider]."
- "THE CONSTRUCTION INDUSTRY'S NATIONWIDE DRONE SERVICE PROVIDER" — Drone Brothers
- "Barnes Creative Studios is the leading provider of high-end video production and immersive virtual tours..." — Barnes
- Credible only with a logo wall behind it. For a solo operator, a warmer variant ("The jobsite video guy [region] GCs call back") fits better.
